How to use this

It is arranged as two sites of tabbed modules, because study design is not read linearly — you arrive needing one design and you want everything about it in one place. Pick a tab and it holds the concept, the algebra, the assumptions, a worked example, the calculator, the pitfalls and the sources for that design alone.

Three things it does differently

WhatWhy
The derivations are visible, and switchableA Derivations toggle in the header hides or reveals the provenance blocks. Formulae are easier to trust and much easier to remember when you have once seen where they came from — but not everyone wants them on every visit.
Pitfalls are interactive, not listed"Non-differential misclassification biases towards the null" is a sentence people nod at and forget. Dragging sensitivity from 100% to 80% and watching an odds ratio of 2.5 collapse to 2.07 is harder to forget, and tells you how much sample size it will cost.

Books are marked check and carry no page numbers, because pagination moves between editions. Every reference has a blank locator box to fill from your own copy, and an export button.

Two corrections worth knowing about

Both are live in the published literature and both are handled correctly here.

  1. The interrupted time series tutorial equation. The 2017 IJE tutorial by Lopez Bernal, Cummins and Gasparrini contained an algebraic definition of the regression model that could lead to erroneous interpretation of the parameters. A corrigendum in 2020 gives the correct form. If you learned interrupted time series from the original before 2020, check which version your code implements.
  2. Matching does not save sample size. It usually costs it, because a matched analysis discards every concordant pair. The explorer on the case–control tab shows the price directly and validates at φ = 0 against the unmatched requirement.

What is deliberately absent

Prediction model sample size, bioequivalence, phase I and dose-finding, N-of-1 designs, SMART designs beyond a mention, and simulation-based power for non-proportional hazards. Each needs real methods rather than a closed form, and a page that pretended otherwise would be worse than one that says so.

Everything here is a design aid. On a complex or high-stakes study it does not replace sitting down with a statistician — it makes that conversation a great deal shorter.
